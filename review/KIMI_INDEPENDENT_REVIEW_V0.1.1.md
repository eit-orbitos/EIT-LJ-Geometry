# Independent Review of EIT_LJ_Geometry_Research_V0.1.1.zip

**Review target:** `EIT_LJ_Geometry_Research_V0.1.1.zip`  
**Review basis:** submitted LaTeX/PDF, Python source, two experiment scripts, full build script, tests, generated data/figures, checksum manifest, licensing files, and release metadata  
**Classification scheme:** A = analytically verified; B = numerically reproduced; C = trial/model parameter; D = heuristic; E = speculative; F = incorrect or unsupported

## 1. VERDICT

# PASS WITH CORRECTIONS

The central reference-model mathematics and numerical results are sound. In particular, the normalization identity, constrained-radial interpretation of the cuboid root, tangential residual, reference large-\(N\) zeta limit, co-spherical bound, and exclusion of \(R=1.3\) within the bound’s stated class all survived independent checking. All committed CSV/JSON files were regenerated byte-for-byte, and all seven figures were regenerated pixel-for-pixel.

A full pass is withheld for five reasons:

1. The general zeta-limit statement omits the necessary condition \(m>2\); `polygon_limit()` also returns meaningless values outside that domain.
2. The abstract/README/conclusion sometimes state the \(N=4\) minimum without explicitly limiting it to the computed \(N=3,\ldots,1000\) scan; no global minimum theorem is proved.
3. The archive still contains the cache and bytecode artifacts that the manuscript and research log say were removed.
4. `normalization_rescale_identity()` mishandles nonzero `source_index` on general, non-vertex-transitive inputs. This does not affect any submitted cuboid result.
5. `CITATION.cff` still contains `REPLACE_WITH_GITHUB_URL` and must be completed before public release.

No affirmative unsupported EIT/H-space or new-physics validation claim remains. The package explicitly disclaims such validation.

## 2. Mathematics review

### 2.1 Pair equilibrium and Lennard–Jones reference — A

From

\[
F(r)=\frac{A}{r^n}-\frac{B}{r^m}=0,\qquad A,B>0,\quad n>m>0,
\]

division by \(B r^{-n}\) gives \((A/B)r^{m-n}=1\), hence

\[
R_{\rm EQ}=\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{1/(n-m)}.
\]

This verifies `equilibrium_radius()` in `src/eit_geometry.py` and `paper/manuscript.tex` lines 41–50. For \(A/B=2,n=13,m=7\), the result is \(2^{1/6}=1.1224620483093729814\ldots\).

For \(V(r)=4(r^{-12}-r^{-6})\),

\[
-V'(r)=24(2r^{-13}-r^{-7}),\qquad
V''(r)=24(26r^{-14}-7r^{-8}).
\]

Thus `lj_force()` and `lj_curvature()` are correct, and the curvature zero is \((26/7)^{1/6}=1.2444550602598078860\ldots\). The exponent pair and coefficient ratio are nevertheless a **reference/model choice (C)**, not an empirical determination of the geometry model; the manuscript says this correctly at lines 52–56.

### 2.2 Geometry-factor root and simplex cancellation — A within the declared model

With \(r_{ij}=R\rho_{ij}\), summing the inward radial projections gives the declared model

\[
F_{\mathcal G}(R)=A R^{-n}G_{\mathcal G}(n)-B R^{-m}G_{\mathcal G}(m).
\]

When \(G_{\mathcal G}(n)\) and \(G_{\mathcal G}(m)\) are positive, its nonzero root is

\[
R_{\rm EQ}^{(\mathcal G)}
=\left[\frac{A G_{\mathcal G}(n)}{B G_{\mathcal G}(m)}\right]^{1/(n-m)}.
\]

This is an analytic consequence of the **declared projected-force model**, not an independently established physical force law. `geometry_equilibrium()` appropriately rejects nonpositive geometry factors.

For a regular simplex under nearest-neighbor normalization, every \(\rho_{ij}=1\). Therefore

\[
G(p)=\sum_{j\ne i}\cos\theta_{ij}
\]

is independent of \(p\), so \(Q=G(n)/G(m)=1\). The triangle and tetrahedron were also independently reproduced. **Class A.**

### 2.3 Polygon formula and the \(N=4\) minimum — B, with scope correction required

The chord and projection derivation in `paper/manuscript.tex` lines 101–113 is correct:

\[
\rho_k=\frac{\sin(\pi k/N)}{\sin(\pi/N)},\qquad
\cos\theta_k=\sin(\pi k/N),
\]

and therefore

\[
G_N(p)=\sin^p(\pi/N)\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sin^{1-p}(\pi k/N).
\]

Independent 80-digit arithmetic gave

\[
R(3)=1.1224620483093729814,\quad
R(4)=1.1126198391757888942,\quad
R(5)=1.1129374683599158358.
\]

The submitted `data/polygon_spectrum_N3_1000.csv` has 998 finite rows, a unique sampled minimum at \(N=4\), and strictly positive successive differences from \(N=4\) through \(N=1000\). Thus the **scan result is B**.

The package is correct to state in `Limitations and Open Problems` that this is specific to the reference \((13,7)\) calculation, not a universal theorem. An independent counterexample confirms non-universality: for \((n,m)=(12,6)\), an \(N=3,\ldots,100\) scan places the minimum at \(N=5\), with \(R\approx1.107351358136681\), rather than \(N=4\).

However, a finite scan plus an asymptotic limit does not prove a global minimum over every integer \(N\ge3\). The abstract, README main-results list, and conclusion should say “within the computed \(N=3,\ldots,1000\) scan.” Without that qualification, a global reading is **F (unsupported)**, not A.

### 2.4 Large-\(N\) zeta limit — A for the reference case; general domain correction required

For fixed \(k\) and \(N\to\infty\),

\[
\sin(\pi/N)\sim\pi/N,\qquad \sin(\pi k/N)\sim\pi k/N.
\]

Each end of the polygon therefore contributes terms asymptotic to \((\pi/N)k^{1-p}\). When \(p>2\), the fixed-\(k\) sums converge and the two ends yield

\[
G_N(p)\sim\frac{2\pi}{N}\zeta(p-1).
\]

Consequently, for \(n>m>2\),

\[
R_\infty=\left[(A/B)\frac{\zeta(n-1)}{\zeta(m-1)}\right]^{1/(n-m)}.
\]

For the reference parameters,

\[
\boxed{R_\infty=[2\zeta(12)/\zeta(6)]^{1/6}
=1.119295879072895603668\ldots}.
\]

Independent high-precision summation gave \(R(1000)=1.1192957642238523581\), agreeing with the submitted float64 value to approximately \(6.5\times10^{-14}\) and lying \(1.14849\times10^{-7}\) below the limit. **Class A for the reference result.**

The manuscript introduces the force model with only \(n>m>0\), but the zeta asymptotic does not hold throughout that larger domain. The condition \(p>2\) is essential: \(\zeta(p-1)\) diverges at \(p=2\), and the endpoint-dominated asymptotic changes for \(p\le2\). The general equation should therefore explicitly assume \(n>m>2\), or separate regimes should be derived.

This is also a code defect. `src/eit_geometry.py:polygon_limit()` has no domain guard. Independent probes produced:

- `(n,m)=(4,2)`: `0.40870126941652`, obtained by substituting a truncated harmonic sum for divergent \(\zeta(1)\);
- `(n,m)=(4,1)`: a complex value.

These do not affect the valid \((13,7)\) result, but the general function is **F outside \(m>2\)** as currently written.

### 2.5 Infinite-chain versus continuum-circle interpretation — A with a precision caveat

The corrected interpretation is geometrically justified. On a unit-circumradius polygon, \(d_{\rm nn}=2\sin(\pi/N)\sim2\pi/N\). If lengths are rescaled to hold \(d_{\rm nn}\) fixed, the circumradius grows proportionally to \(N\), and the vertices at fixed index separation \(k\) approach points spaced by \(k\) on a straight chain.

There is an important qualification: for fixed \(k\), the inward radial projection scales as \(\pi k/N\), so it tends to zero. Hence \(G_N(p)=O(1/N)\), and the coefficient \(\zeta(p-1)\) includes this curvature weighting. It is not the conventional infinite-chain sum with unit radial projection, which would produce \(2\zeta(p)\). Nor is it a finite-radius continuum-circle force integral; for the reference exponents, the near-source continuum singularity would not supply this finite zeta ratio.

Thus “nearest-neighbor-rescaled **local** infinite-chain limit” is acceptable **A**, but one additional sentence about the vanishing/curvature-weighted projection would prevent a misleading conventional-chain reading.

### 2.6 Co-spherical bound and exclusion of \(R=1.3\) — A under the stated assumptions

Assume nearest-neighbor normalization, \(1\le\rho_j\le\rho_{\max}\), nonnegative projection weights \(w_j\), and \(n>m>0\). Then

\[
\rho_{\max}^{-(n-m)}\rho_j^{-m}
\le \rho_j^{-n}\le\rho_j^{-m}.
\]

Multiplying by \(w_j\ge0\) and summing proves

\[
\rho_{\max}^{-(n-m)}G(m)\le G(n)\le G(m),
\]

so

\[
\rho_{\max}^{-(n-m)}\le Q\le1.
\]

Taking the positive \(1/(n-m)\) power gives exactly

\[
\boxed{R_{\rm ref}/\rho_{\max}\le R_{\rm EQ}^{(\mathcal G)}\le R_{\rm ref}}.
\]

For \(A/B=2,n=13,m=7\),

\[
R_{\rm EQ}^{(\mathcal G)}\le2^{1/6}=1.1224620483\ldots<1.3.
\]

Therefore the bound really does exclude \(R=1.3\) **within the reference, nearest-neighbor-normalized, co-spherical class (A)**. It does not exclude 1.3 under fixed-edge normalization, for arbitrary non-co-spherical geometries, or for arbitrary \(A/B\); the corrected manuscript generally respects this limitation. The nonnegative-projection assumption is in fact automatic for points on a sphere centered at the source’s centroid, but retaining it is harmless.

### 2.7 Cuboid normalization identity — A and B

For fixed vertices and source, changing \(d_{\rm ref}\) from \(d_{\rm nn}\) to \(d_{\rm fixed}\) gives

\[
\rho_{ij}^{\rm fixed}=\rho_{ij}^{\rm near}\frac{d_{\rm nn}}{d_{\rm fixed}},
\qquad
G_{\rm fixed}(p)=\left(\frac{d_{\rm fixed}}{d_{\rm nn}}\right)^pG_{\rm near}(p).
\]

Substitution into the radial-root formula yields

\[
\boxed{R_{\rm fixed}=R_{\rm near}\frac{d_{\rm fixed}}{d_{\rm nn}}}.
\]

This is exact, not a numerical approximation. `data/cuboid_normalization_sensitivity.csv` confirms it over 601 cuboids with maximum float64 absolute error \(1.7763568394002505\times10^{-15}\). **Class A, with B confirmation.**

A separate API bug remains: `normalization_rescale_identity()` accepts `source_index`, uses it to calculate `d_nn`, but calls `geometry_equilibrium()` without passing it; `geometry_equilibrium()` itself always uses source 0. On asymmetric inputs, the reported identity error can therefore be spurious. All submitted cuboids are vertex-transitive and use source 0, so their results are unaffected.

### 2.8 Cuboid minimum and tangential residual — B

Independent closed-shell summation over the seven other cuboid vertices reproduced

\[
\delta_{\rm grid}=0.8513399225207845,\qquad
R_{\rm grid}=1.0472520792319080457.
\]

This is the minimum of the stated 409-point scan. A separate continuous optimization found \(\delta\approx0.8517671273107278\), \(R\approx1.0472517021752092\), which is consistent with the manuscript’s wording “minimum near” over a finite scan.

At the submitted grid minimum, an independent 80-digit vector-force calculation with prefactor 24 gave

\[
\mathbf F_{\rm net}=(-3.3710763464328979,-3.3710763464328979,7.9194602702320633),
\]

zero radial component to working precision, and

\[
\boxed{\|\mathbf F_{\rm tang}\|=9.2437093441292741797}.
\]

This matches `data/summary_results.json` (`9.243709344129282`) to float64 accuracy. The reported root is therefore **not a full vector equilibrium** at this deformed shape; “constrained radial equilibrium” is the correct interpretation. **Class B; the old full-equilibrium interpretation is F.**

### 2.9 Thresholds and physical interpretation — D/F

The endpoints \(0.9\) and \(1.3\) remain **D (heuristic)**. Neither the LJ zero, the curvature transition, nor the geometry scans derives both endpoints. Any claim that the package experimentally validates EIT/H-space, proves a new force law, or establishes new physics is **F**. No such affirmative claim remains in the submitted manuscript or README.

## 3. Computational reproduction

### 3.1 Procedure

The archive was CRC-tested and extracted without executing its compiled files. A separate clean-build copy was made; `data/`, `figures/`, `.pytest_cache/`, and `__pycache__/` contents were removed before rebuilding. The following were run from the clean copy:

- `scripts/build_all.py`;
- `experiments/experiment_01_geometry_spectrum.py`;
- `experiments/experiment_02_cuboid_deformation.py`;
- the supplied nine-test regression suite.

Review environment: Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.2.5, Matplotlib 3.10.3, mpmath 1.3.0, and pytest 9.2.0. Independent high-precision calculations did not call the package’s geometry-factor or residual functions.

The supplied tests are useful regression checks, not independent proof of the mathematics. In particular, they do not test the \(m\le2\) zeta domain, global polygon behavior beyond \(N=1000\), or nonzero-source behavior on asymmetric configurations.

## 6. Remaining required corrections

### Required before GitHub/Zenodo

1. **Correct the large-\(N\) domain.** In `paper/manuscript.tex` lines 132–150 and the `polygon_limit()` docstring, state \(n>m>2\) for the zeta formula. Add validation to `polygon_limit()` so \(m\le2\) raises an informative error instead of returning a truncated-harmonic or complex result. If the full \(n>m>0\) regime is desired, derive and test the separate \(m<2\), \(m=2\), and \(m>2\) asymptotics.

2. **Scan-qualify the \(N=4\) claim.** Change unqualified occurrences in the abstract, `README.md` main results, and conclusion to “unique minimum within the computed \(N=3,\ldots,1000\) scan.” A global theorem requires a separate proof covering all \(N>1000\).

3. **Actually remove the artifacts.** Delete `.pytest_cache/`, `src/__pycache__/`, and `tests/__pycache__/` from the release archive, then regenerate `MANIFEST.sha256`. This will make `manuscript.tex` line 263, `RESEARCH_LOG.md` line 15, and correction 8 true.

4. **Fix source-index propagation.** Add `source_index` to `geometry_equilibrium()` and pass it to both `geometry_factor()` calls. Use it in `normalization_rescale_identity()` and in the `actual` evaluation inside `spherical_nearest_bounds()`. Add an asymmetric-geometry regression test. The submitted source-0 results do not need numerical revision.

5. **Complete release metadata.** Replace `CITATION.cff`’s `repository-code: "REPLACE_WITH_GITHUB_URL"` with the real repository URL, compile and inspect the final PDF, and regenerate hashes after all edits.

### Recommended but nonblocking

- Add one sentence explaining that the local-chain radial projections vanish as \(O(1/N)\), making the zeta coefficient curvature-weighted.
- Pin exact dependency versions if binary-identical figures are part of the reproducibility promise.
- Include the full PolyForm and CC license texts, or have the current URL-based notices reviewed for the intended distribution method.
- Remove or cite the uncited Shannon entry in `paper/references.bib` and the manual bibliography.
- Add high-precision regression tests for the zeta limit, cuboid residual, and nonzero-source API behavior.
- Keep the archived Tasklet document labeled as an author-supplied AI-review summary, not scientific peer review.

## 7. Publication readiness

# READY WITH MINOR EDITS

The package is **not ready to upload unchanged**, but the required changes are localized and do not overturn the reference results. After the five required corrections above, a clean rebuild, nine-plus regression tests, PDF inspection, and manifest refresh, the package should be suitable for **GitHub + Zenodo** as a theoretical/computational preprint.

It should not be presented as experimentally validated physics, a universal theory of geometry-dependent equilibria, or a derivation of the \([0.9,1.3]\) interval.
